# D=12: the two dimensions without Magma

This establishes the full adelic, finite-level-one dimensions

\[
\dim S_4(1)=2,\qquad \dim S_5(\varepsilon)=1
\]

over \(F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt3)\). It uses published ring structures, followed by
small integer coefficient counts. The exact coefficient counts and the
Dirichlet special values determining the scalar 12 are reproduced in
`verify_d12_weight_one.py`. The polynomial-ring theorems, component
identification, and constant-term ranks remain mathematical inputs, as
specified in its JSON output.

Both refer to K.-B. Gundlach, *Die Bestimmung der Funktionen zu einigen
Hilbertschen Modulgruppen*, J. reine angew. Math. **220** (1965), 109–153.
Here we use the precise decompositions restated above, rather than identify
an unspecified classical space with an adelic space.

Write \(u=2+\sqrt3\). In Aoki's notation, the first sign in \(A_k^{ab}\)
is the Galois-symmetry sign, and the second is the sign under
\((z_1,z_2)\mapsto(uz_1,u'z_2)\). On \(\mathfrak H^2\), his results give

\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\dim(A_k^{++}\oplus A_k^{-+})x^k
=\frac{1+x^{11}}{(1-x^2)(1-x^3)(1-x^4)}.
\]

For the mixed domain \(\mathfrak H\times\mathfrak H^-\), Aoki §4.1 gives
the full \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) series
\((1+x^{10})/((1-x)(1-x^3)(1-x^4))\).
Dursthoff gives its Galois-symmetric ring as \(\mathbf C[g_1,s_3,g_4]\),
and the subring also invariant under \(u\) as
\(\mathbf C[g_1,g_4,g_6]\). The subscripts are the weights.
The latter generators are Eisenstein series. On \(\mathfrak H^2\), the
corresponding polynomial generators of weights \(2,3,4\) are Eisenstein
series as well.

Aoki Theorem 2.2 has a typographical error in its final superscript, where
`++` should read `--`. The required second-sign-plus summands are the first
and third lines, agreeing with Theorem 2.1's table. No inference here uses
the erroneous final line.

## Matching the adelic spaces

The ordinary class number is one: Minkowski's bound is \(\sqrt{12}/2<2\).
There is no negative-norm unit, since \(a^2-3b^2=-1\) is impossible modulo
3. The units are \(\{\pm u^m:m\in\mathbf Z\}\), and the narrow class
number is two, represented by \(\mathcal O_F\) and \((\sqrt3)\).

The usual adelic decomposition at full level therefore has two classical
components. They are the two sign types of the full modular group, acting
on \(\mathfrak H^2\) and \(\mathfrak H\times\mathfrak H^-\).
Indeed an ideal in either component is principal; conjugation by a diagonal
matrix with its generator identifies the corresponding ideal group with
\(\mathrm{GL}_2^+(\mathcal O_F)\). A generator with signs \((+,-)\)
changes the second half-plane. In the manuscript's different-shifted
convention, \(\mathfrak d_F=(2\sqrt3)\), so the two ideals
\(t_\nu\mathfrak d_F\) are represented by \((2\sqrt3)\) and \((6)\).
Thus the principal narrow component, with shifted ideal `(2 sqrt(3))`,
is the mixed domain, and the nonprincipal component, with shifted ideal
`(6)`, is the upper-upper domain. This assignment is also used in the
weight-one normalization and support argument below.

Modulo scalar matrices,
\(\mathrm{GL}_2^+(\mathcal O_F)\) is generated by
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal O_F)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(u,1)\).
For parallel weight the latter's determinant factor is
\(N(u)^{k/2}=1\). Thus its forms are precisely the **second-sign-plus**
spaces above. No Galois-symmetry restriction is imposed on the adelic space.

The central character is also determined. If an everywhere finite-unramified
character has the required parallel-weight infinity type, its finite value
on \((\alpha)\) must be \(\operatorname{sgn}(N\alpha)^k\). This is
well-defined because all units have positive norm, and every ideal is
principal. It is the trivial character for even \(k\), and the nontrivial
narrow class character \(\varepsilon\) for odd \(k\). Equivalently two
characters of the required infinity type could differ only by an ordinary
class character, which here is trivial. The component sum consequently
has exactly the manuscript's character, with no additional character
sectors to remove.

## Dimensions and cuspidality

On \(\mathfrak H^2\), the weight-4 monomials are \(G_2^2,G_4\),
and the weight-5 monomial is \(G_2G_3\). Their independence follows from
the polynomial-ring theorem. Its antisymmetric summand starts at weight
11. Thus the two modular dimensions are exactly \(2,1\).

On the mixed domain, the full \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) series and the
Galois-symmetric ring both have dimensions \(3,3\) in weights \(4,5\):
the latter bases are \(g_1^4,g_1s_3,g_4\) and
\(g_1^5,g_1^2s_3,g_1g_4\), respectively, and the extra generator of
weight 10 cannot occur. Thus there are no Galois-antisymmetric forms in
these weights. Its full
\(\mathrm{GL}_2^+\) space therefore equals the doubly invariant subspace
in these two weights. The ring \(\mathbf C[g_1,g_4,g_6]\) has the
weight-4 basis \(g_1^4,g_4\) and weight-5 basis \(g_1^5,g_1g_4\).
Both modular dimensions are 2.

Each classical group has one cusp, since \(\mathcal O_F\) is a PID and
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal O_F)\) acts transitively on
\(\mathbf P^1(F)\). The constant-term map has rank one in both weights:
on the upper-upper component use the products of Eisenstein generators
of weights \(2+2\) and \(2+3\); on the mixed component use \(g_1^k\).
Hence one subtracts exactly one on each component:

| Weight | Upper-upper modular | Mixed modular | Upper-upper cusp | Mixed cusp | Full adelic cusp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| 5 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 1 | 1 |

This proves both required dimension statements. The mathematical inputs
are the published ring theorems; the displayed monomials and the
constant-term argument complete the dimension calculation in the text.

The full mixed-domain SL2 modular dimensions `3,3` are taken before
imposing unit invariance. They are not the principal-component modular
dimensions of the full adelic spaces. The latter are `2,2`. The full
adelic modular dimensions are `4,3`, and the full adelic Eisenstein
dimension is two in both weights.

## The weight-one normalization and support

Artin factorization for the CM extension
`Q(zeta_12)/Q(sqrt(3))` gives

\[
L(0,\varepsilon)
=L(0,\chi_{-4})L(0,\chi_{-3})
=\frac12\cdot\frac13=\frac16.
\]

The two rational Dirichlet values follow from
`L(0,chi) = -sum_{a=1}^q chi(a)(a/q-1/2)`. The primitive character
tables modulo four and three are `(0,1,0,-1)` and `(0,1,-1)`,
respectively. Exact rational evaluation yields the displayed values.

For `E=E_1(1,epsilon)`, the weight-one constant formula therefore gives

\[
c_\nu(0,E)=\frac{1+\varepsilon(t_\nu)}{24}.
\]

Its constant term is `1/12` on the principal mixed component and zero
on the nonprincipal upper-upper component. Every nonzero ideal
coefficient on the latter is also zero. Indeed, for an ideal `a` with
`epsilon(a)=-1`, the divisor involution `r -> a/r` changes the sign
of `epsilon(r)` and has no fixed point. Thus
`sum_{r|a} epsilon(r)=0`. This is an argument for all ideals, rather
than a finite coefficient experiment.

A normalized weight-four eigenform has principal-component
unit-ideal coefficient one. That component's cusp space has dimension
one, so the two normalized weight-four eigenforms have identical
principal restrictions. Their other components are annihilated by `E`.
Their products consequently agree and have unit-ideal coefficient
`1/12`. Since the full target `S_5(epsilon)` has dimension one,

\[
h_5=12E_1(1,\varepsilon)h_4.
\]

The exact arithmetic reproduces the low-weight counts and the scalar;
the ring theorems and the preceding component-support argument identify
the modular forms. No reconstruction of a Hilbert modular form or
finite Fourier test for the global identity is claimed.
